## Summary

### 1. What a bond is

When you buy a bond, you pay an issue price today, and in return you get regular coupon payments (say every year or twice a year) and then the final redemption payment, which is usually the face value, at the expiry date. The face value is the amount that the coupon rate and the redemption are based on. If the bond pays coupons m times a year, each payment is the annual coupon (coupon rate x face value) split into m payments (note that the coupon rate is seperate from the market interest rate). At the end, you receive the redemption payment, R times the face value. Most bonds are redeemable at par (R=1). A zero coupon bond skips the regular coupons and just pays one lump sum at expiry.
